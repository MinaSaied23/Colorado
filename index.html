the prompt

One of the benefits of Colorado College’s Block Plan is the opportunity to immerse yourself fully in a single subject for 3.5 weeks. We see this as the luxury of focus — the joy and value of directing your full attention to one thing. Tell us about a time when you experienced this kind of deep focus in an academic or extracurricular setting. What were you doing and how did it turn out?

⏱ ~1.5 min read 246 words 💡 hover underlined phrases

One question,
all of me.

Last year, in eleventh-grade biology, the class was learning how a nerve fires. I asked the opposite question: with so much activity in the body, how does the brain know what to ignore?

I couldn't put it down. For three and a half weeks, I lived inside it — reading until I understood the noise, building a closed-loop core-conductor model of ephaptic crosstalk, testing how space and time sum the interference when a nerve is compressed. I rebuilt the model more times than I can count. Everything else went quiet.

The result was a sole-authored paper now in second-round review at the Journal of Computational Neuroscience. What stayed with me wasn't the result. It was the quiet: one question, all of me, for as long as it took. That is the luxury I want a Block Plan for.

how it turned out Sole author · second-round peer review · Springer Nature
— Mina
